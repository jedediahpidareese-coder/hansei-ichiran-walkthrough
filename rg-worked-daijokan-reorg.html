42  The Daijōkan reorganization （太政官三院制）

official chronicle entry summarizing an imperial administrative decree (『明治史要』本紀 annals entry; the underlying act was a 太政官布告 / Daijōkan proclamation) · 明治四年七月二十九日 / 1871-09-13

This is the chronicle entry recording the decree of Meiji 4/7/29 (13 September 1871) that rebuilt the Daijōkan into the three-chamber Sei-in / Sa-in / U-in system, just fifteen days after the abolition of the domains (haihan chiken). It names Sanjō Sanetomi as Grand Minister of State and the four sangi councillors — Saigō, Kidō, Ōkuma, Itagaki — so it is the primary-source anchor for the whole post-abolition central government. It is an excellent reading exercise because it is dense with Meiji administrative vocabulary and organ names while being grammatically compact: almost every clause ends in a bare 漢文訓読体 verb (更定シ, 置キ, 任ス, 置ク, plus the classical instrumental frame ～ヲ以テ～ト爲ス), so once a reader can decode this passage they can read the surrounding institutional record. Note that the Meiji Shiyō entry is a chronicle SUMMARY of the imperial action, not the verbatim edict text; the fully verbatim 太政官布告 would be sought in 法令全書, which is outside this workspace.

42.2 Grammar notes

文語 form What it is / modern bridge
連用形 + シ／キ chaining (更定シ…置キ…) In 漢文訓読体, a verb in its 連用形 (continuative) with no conjunction just runs on to the next clause, the way modern Japanese uses ～て. 更定シ = 更定して, 置キ = 置いて. You already do this in modern Japanese with the て-form; here the て is simply absent and the raw 連用形 does the joining.
サ変終止形 任ス／置ク (bare sentence-final verb) Classical/kanbun-style sentences end in the plain 終止形 with no です/ます and often no stated subject: 任ス (‘[X] appoints/is appointed’), 置ク (‘[X] places’). 任ス is the 文語 サ変 form (任ず) of modern 任じる/任命する, written with unvoiced ス but read にんず. Read the subject from context — here it is ‘the government / the decree’.
～ヲ以テ～ト爲ス (instrumental appointment frame) The core ‘appoint A as B’ pattern of Meiji decrees. 以テ (をもって) literally ‘taking/by means of’, 爲ス (なす) ‘makes [it] be’. So 三條實美ヲ以テ太政大臣（ト爲ス）= ‘takes Sanjō Sanetomi and makes him Grand Minister’ = modern 三条実美を太政大臣とする/に任命する. In this compressed chronicle the closing ト爲ス is dropped, leaving just ～ヲ以テ [office] or even ～ヲ [office].
文語 adverbial 新ニ 新なり is the 文語 形容動詞 ‘to be new’; its 連用形 新に functions adverbially = modern 新たに (‘newly’). The ～ニ here is the classical adverbial ending, NOT the case particle に (contrast 一等ニ任ス, where ニ IS the case particle に).
等 = など / ～ら The single character 等 does the work of modern など (after things: 大少史等 = ‘Dai- and Shō-shi and so forth’) and of ～ら (after people: 板垣退助等 = ‘板垣退助ら’, ‘Itagaki Taisuke and the others’). Same character, two modern equivalents depending on whether it follows things or people.
katakana okurigana / particles (ヲ, ニ, ノ, シ, キ) Meiji official prose writes all okurigana and particles in katakana against a base of 漢字. It is not a different grammar — just a script convention. Mentally swap ヲ→を, ニ→に, ノ→の, and read verb-tails like ～シ, ～キ, ～ス as ordinary conjugation endings.

Uncertain readings in this passage

  • The three bureaus placed in the Sei-in are only partially legible in the scan. The source transcription (line 27) prints them as [主計?][皇學?]雅樂 and the 2026-05-26 audit (source lines 3, 27) states that at least the first two characters are not fully readable; 主計 (accounting) and 皇學 (imperial studies) are the audit’s best guesses, with 雅樂 (court music) the one confident member. These readings are marked tentative in the tokens above and should not be treated as settled.
  • The trailing string 「雜ノ所スル所」 at the end of source line 27 does not parse as coherent 文語 and appears to be a mis-read or obscured tail in the white-on-black JP2 scan. It is preserved verbatim as genbun but left untranslated; its intended text is unknown.
  • The official-rank string 「大史正權大少史等」 (source line 25) is a compressed chronicle enumeration. I have segmented it as 大史 / 正・權の大史・少史 / 等, but the exact intended list of graded titles (which are 正, which are 權, how 大/少 distribute) cannot be fixed from this compressed summary alone; the source’s own working English (line 35) renders it loosely as ‘Daishi, Shōken-Daishi, Shōshi, and so forth.’ The reading しょうごんだいしょうし for 正權大少史 is a plausible reconstruction, not a settled reading.
  • The phrase 「諸官省ヲ統括ノ…」 (source line 25) is grammatically compressed. I read it as ‘oversees all the ministries’, but whether 統括 attaches to the Sei-in as subject-organ or modifies the following rank list is not fully determinable from the chronicle wording; the source treats it loosely as ‘to centralize all ministries, with the official ranks…’.
  • The 議長／議員 of clause 4 are historically the Speaker and members of the Sa-in, but the source genbun (line 26) does not itself name which chamber; the chamber attribution is contextual inference, not stated in the text.
  • The closing ト爲ス of the two appointment clauses (三條實美ヲ以テ太政大臣 / …等ヲ参議) is not printed in the chronicle; it is supplied as understood from the standard ～ヲ以テ～ト爲ス frame and from the parallel structure of the two lines. The genbun as printed ends at 太政大臣 and 参議 respectively.
  • The source explicitly notes (lines 3, 157–159) that the personnel/bureau list following 西郷隆盛 is partly obscured in the scan and that this Meiji Shiyō entry is a chronicle SUMMARY of the imperial action, not the verbatim 詔勅/太政官布告 text; the fully verbatim edict would be sought in 法令全書, which is outside this workspace.
  • The draft’s original kanji_pairs included 「參／参議 → 参」 and 「藏（大蔵）→ 蔵」. 大蔵 (藏) does NOT appear anywhere in this chapter’s quoted genbun (lines 25–29); it occurs only in the surrounding-day entries (source lines 48, 50), so the 藏 pair was removed as out of scope for this passage. For 参議, the source line 29 prints the character in the already-modern form 参 (not the historical 旧字体 參), so no old→new substitution is actually present in the text; this is noted at the 参議 token rather than asserted as a kanji_pair.
