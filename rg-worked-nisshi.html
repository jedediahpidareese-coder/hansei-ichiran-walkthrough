45  A domain debt petition （太政官日誌の上申）

Official gazette entry (太政官日誌) reproducing a domain governor’s petition (上申／上表) in 候文 epistolary-documentary style, followed by the imperial approval (御沙汰) · 明治三年九月五日（庚午九月五日） / 1870-09-29 (petition signed); issue #38 spans Meiji 3/9/5–9/10 ≈ Sept 29 – Oct 4, 1870

This is the anchor case of the 1870–71 domain debt-relief petitions: Kumamoto governor Hosokawa Morihisa asks the Meiji government to let him keep only half of the one-tenth of domain rice granted to him as a household stipend, redirecting the other half to poor-relief inside the domain — and the government’s one-line approval is printed right below it. It is an ideal 候文 (sororobun) worked example because in a few lines it stacks nearly every marker N2 readers have not met: the humble verb 奉存候, the passive-honorific 被〜候, the desiderative 〜度, the fixed epistolary sign-off 恐惶敬白, and a 干支 (sexagenary) date 庚午. Because the workspace transcription is explicitly partial (two “…” gaps and at least two likely OCR misreads), it is also a realistic exercise in reading a flawed source honestly rather than smoothing it over.

45.2 Grammar notes

文語 form What it is / modern bridge
候文（そうろうぶん）— 〜候 as the polite sentence-ender In this epistolary-documentary register, 候 (sōrō) is attached at the end of predicates as the neutral polite copula/verb-ender, roughly where modern Japanese uses 〜ます／です／〜ている. So 奉存候 = modern 存じております, 奉懇願候 = 懇願しております, 被聞食届候(事) = お聞き届けになった(こと). Read 候 as ‘polite finish here,’ not as the verb ‘to wait/serve.’
被〜（る／らる）— passive-honorific auxiliary written 被 文語 る／らる (=modern れる／られる) is written with the character 被 placed BEFORE the verb it modifies (kanbun word order). It carries two of its classical senses here: honorific (elevating the emperor) in 被聞食届 = ‘deigns to hear and grant,’ and receptive/honorific in 被成下 = ‘graciously does [for me].’ N2 already knows れる／られる do both passive and respect; 被 is just the documentary way of writing them. Note it is passive/honorific, NOT causative.
〜度（たし）— desiderative auxiliary, 連用形 たく 文語 たし = modern 〜たい (‘want to’). It appears as 度 in 被成下度 (なしくだされたく = ‘I wish that it be graciously granted’) and, standardly across these petitions, as 差向度／供度 (‘wish to allocate’). Seeing 〜度 near the end of a clause is the signal that a request is being made.
奉〜候（たてまつり…そうろう）— humble-verb stacking 奉る is the humble auxiliary (=modern 〜し申し上げる／お〜する). Stacked with 候 it forms deferential ‘I humbly VERB’: 奉存候 = 存じ上げております, 奉懇願候 = 懇願し申し上げます. Recognize 奉 + noun/verb + 候 as one humble unit.
之 = classical genitive の Throughout the passage 之 is simply the classical/kanbun way of writing の: 十分之一 = 十分の一, 恐懼之至 = 恐懼の至り, 願之通 = 願いの通り, 申立之趣 = 申し立ての趣. Substitute の on sight.
ニ付（につき）— documentary causal connective 文語 〜に付き = modern 〜ので／〜のため (‘because, owing to’). 恐懼之至ニ付 = ‘(because it is) the utmost trepidation, therefore…’; 尤之儀ニ付 = ‘because it is a reasonable matter, therefore…’. It links a reason to the action that follows.
連用形 for clause-chaining (供シ, 家用トシ) 文語 chains clauses with the 連用形 (continuative), where modern Japanese would use the 〜て form. 家用トシ = 家用として(‘makes it household use, and…’), 民撫恤之用ニ供シ = 供して(‘provides it for relief, and…’). Read a bare 連用形 mid-sentence as ‘VERB, and then.’
〜事（こと）as a decree-final nominalizer In the imperial approval, 候事 ends the ruling. Attaching 事 to a 候-predicate turns the whole statement into an official disposition — ‘it is [hereby settled] that…’. This 〜べき事／〜候事 pattern is the standard closing of Meiji官文 (official) decisions and directives.
干支（えと／かんし）dating — 庚午 The date is given by the sexagenary cycle, not a regnal year: 庚午 (kanoe-uma, ‘metal-horse’) = Meiji 3 = 1870; the next year Meiji 4 is 辛未 (kanoto-hitsuji). N2 readers know 干支 only from zodiac animals; here it is the actual calendar, so 庚午九月五日 = Meiji 3, 9th month, 5th day.

Uncertain readings in this passage

  • The source file explicitly labels this a ‘Working transcription from canvases 43-44, partial; full transcription pending higher-resolution re-download’ (line 66). The whole genbun block should be treated as provisional, not a verified reading.
  • Two ‘…’ ellipses in the source (after 奉存候然處, and after 民撫恤之用ニ供シ) mark text the transcription omitted. The clause units here therefore skip elided material; the petition’s actual wording between these points is not captured in the file.
  • 譲: the source clause opens 譲支配地現石十分之一 — a leading 譲 before 支配地 whose sense is unclear (possibly ‘the territory ceded/entrusted to my governance’, possibly an OCR/segmentation artifact). The genbun is copied verbatim, but 譲 is not confidently translatable from this partial transcription and both this chapter’s kakikudashi/English and the source file’s own working English leave it unrendered.
  • 家祿トシラ: printed トシラ is almost certainly a slip/OCR error for トシテ (=として). The genbun is copied verbatim as トシラ, but the intended reading is ‘as [a household stipend].’
  • 下賜天恩隆渥: the file runs 下賜 and 天恩隆渥 together with no break and no explicit passive auxiliary attached to 下賜; the segmentation into ‘(it) was granted; the imperial favor is deep’ and the receptive reading of 下賜 are interpretive, and a verb such as 被下 (くだされ) may be elided at the seam.
  • 致仕合奉存候: the 致 character sits between 天恩隆渥 and 仕合奉存候 in the verbatim genbun; it is read here as 連用形 いたし linking into 仕合…奉存候, but its exact function in the partial text is not certain.
  • 充陵儀: the character 陵 after 充 is almost certainly a misread. Read the sense as 充つる(儀)/充候 = ‘the matter of applying [the stipend] to household use.’ The exact character cannot be confidently recovered from this transcription.
  • The imperial-approval line is given in the file in two forms — the search-heuristic/co-publication summary uses 本文申立之趣尤之儀ニ付願之通被聞食届候事, while the same series elsewhere prints the variant 書面申立之趣… and 願之通被仰付候事. This chapter follows the 本文…被聞食届候事 form as printed for the Kumamoto approval (lines 56, 96).
  • 護久 is the personal name of 細川護久 (Hosokawa Morihisa); the surname 細川 is supplied from the file’s identification (lines 47, 106) and is not written in the signature block itself.
  • Readings given for 恐懼之至 (きょうくのいたり), 天恩隆渥 (てんおんりゅうあく), and 民撫恤 (みんぶじゅつ) are standard on-yomi for these set phrases; the source does not furigana them, so vowel-length details are editorial.
