43  The stipend buyout （金禄公債證書發行條例）

imperial-government proclamation (太政官布告) with attached ordinance (條例) · 明治九年八月五日 / 1876-08-05

This is the opening of the 1876 秩禄処分 (stipend settlement) — the Daijōkan proclamation No. 108 that abolished hereditary samurai and merit stipends and replaced them with one-time public-debt bonds, together with Article 1 of the attached issuance ordinance. It is an ideal reading exercise because it is compact but shows every hallmark of Meiji legal-ordinance style at once: the 布告 proclamation frame with its 候文 (sōrō-bun) verb endings (候處・候條・候事), the katakana-okurigana convention (ヲ・ノ・トモ・スヘシ), numbered-article structure (第一條), 旧字体 throughout (號・祿・證・條・發), and the classical imperative べし. The famous universal-coverage clause 「華士族及ヒ平民トモ」 — “kazoku, shizoku, or commoner alike” — is the single most-quoted line of the decree, so learning to decode it here is directly useful.

43.2 Grammar notes

文語 form What it is / modern bridge
候文 (そうろうぶん) — 候 as polite auxiliary 候 (そうろう) is the classical polite copula/auxiliary that ends nearly every clause of official Meiji prose; it occupies the slot where modern Japanese uses です/ます or plain である. You already know 〜ます as the polite register — read 候 the same way, just older and more formal. It rarely stands alone; it fuses with connectives (候處・候條・候事).
〜候處 (そうろうところ) 候 + 處(=処)ところ. This is the concessive/transition ‘X was the case, but/whereupon…’. Bridges to modern 「〜であったところ／〜したが」. In this decree 「給與有之候處」 = ‘stipends had been granted this way, however—’, pivoting to the new rule.
〜候條 (そうろうじょう) 候 + 條 used causally = ‘since/because, therefore’. Do NOT read this 條 as ‘article number’ — here it means ‘grounds/reason’. Bridges to modern 「〜するので／したので」. 「下賜候條」 = ‘since (they) shall be conferred, therefore—’ leading into the proclamation formula.
〜候事 (そうろうこと) — decree-closing formula The fixed ending of a 布告. 「布告候事」 = ‘this is hereby proclaimed / it is so ordered’. Functionally a performative full-stop; N2 learners can treat 「〜候事」 as the legal equivalent of ending a notice with 「〜とする。」
べし (here スヘシ=すべし, 付與スヘシ) Classical auxiliary of conjecture/obligation attached to the 終止形. In statutes it is the mandatory ‘shall/must’. Bridges to modern べきだ / 〜するものとする / 〜せよ. 「付與スヘシ」 = ‘shall be conferred’. Note the katakana okurigana: ス=す, ヘ=べ, シ=し.
〜ヲ以テ (をもって) 文語 instrumental ‘by means of / on the basis of’, = modern によって/で. Appears twice: 「年限等ヲ以テ」 (on the basis of duration-limits) and 「公債證書ヲ以テ」 (by means of bond certificates).
有之 (これあり) — 候文 word-order inversion Written 有之 but read これあり (= 之(これ)有り). Kanbun-influenced official prose inverts object and verb; you read the lower character first. = modern (〜が)あった/存在した. 「給與有之候處」 = ‘there had been such grants, but—’.
〜トモ (list + とも = ‘all alike / regardless’) 文語 とも appended after a listed set makes it universal-inclusive: ‘both/all of them, no matter which’. 「華士族及ヒ平民トモ」 = ‘kazoku, shizoku AND commoner alike’. Bridges to modern 「〜も（いずれも）／〜を問わず」. This is the grammatical engine of the universal-coverage clause.
及ヒ (および) — 文語 conjunction with katakana okurigana 及び ‘and’, but the okurigana is written in katakana (ヒ = び) per the Meiji official convention where okurigana on kanji were set in katakana, not hiragana. Read exactly as modern および.
連用形 chaining (改メ, 下渡ス…コト) Classical prose chains clauses with the 連用形 (stem form) instead of the modern て-form. 「制限ヲ改メ…」 = ‘amends the conditions, and…’. When you see a bare stem (改め, 給し, 据置き) ending a clause, read it as modern 〜て/〜し and keep going — the sentence is not finished.
旧字体 + katakana-okurigana orthography (general) Two surface conventions run through the whole passage: (1) pre-1946 kanji forms (號 for 号, 祿 for 禄, 證 for 証, 條 for 条, 發 for 発, 與 for 与, 處 for 処, 來 for 来); (2) grammatical particles and okurigana written in KATAKANA (ノ=の, ヲ=を, ヘ=へ/べ, ヨリ=より, トモ=とも). N2 readers know all these words in hiragana/新字体 — the task is only to re-map the surface glyphs. (One inconsistency: the ordinance title prints 禄 in 新字体 while the body prints 祿; the transcription copies each as printed.)

Uncertain readings in this passage

  • Clause 「一時ニ之ヲ下渡スコト爲ニ以テ」: the printed word order 「爲ニ以テ」 is a stiff bureaucratic purpose phrase reproduced verbatim from the source file (confirmed identical in BOTH source files: transcription line 73 and qmd line 15). Its exact parse is somewhat awkward; I read it as ‘in order to (do so)’ ≈ modern 〜するために/〜すべく. The English rendering treats the whole clause as a purpose clause (‘in order to hand them over at once’), which matches the source file’s own translation (‘in order to redeem them at one time’, transcription line 79). The literal internal grammar of 爲ニ以テ is not fully transparent and a specialist may parse it differently; the reading ためにもって is a plausible gloss, not source-attested.
  • Orthographic inconsistency in the source between 祿 and 禄: the proclamation body and Article 1 print the 旧字体 家祿・賞典祿・給與 (with 祿), while the ordinance TITLE as transcribed reads 金禄公債證書發行條例 (with the 新字体-looking 禄). VERIFIED: this inconsistency is present in the source file itself (line 71 title uses 禄; lines 47 and 73 body use 祿). Each occurrence has been copied exactly as the source has it, without normalizing. Whether the title’s 禄 reflects the actual printed glyph on the NDL image or a transcription normalization by the source file’s author is not independently verifiable from the transcription alone.
  • All on/kun readings (えいせい, かし, ふよ, くだしわたす, きゅうよ, etc.) are standard dictionary readings supplied for the N2 reader; the source file (a transcription, not a furigana edition) does not itself vouch for readings, only for the character string. Readings are a scholarly gloss, not primary-source-attested. 給與 is glossed きゅうよ (現代 給与); the 與→与 pair is read よ in the kanji_pairs table.
  • The full-width space inside 「（八月五日　輪郭附）」 and after 「第一條　」 is preserved from the source file’s transcription; it represents printed spacing in the original and is reproduced verbatim in the genbun. Both full-width spaces were confirmed present in the source (lines 45 and 73).
